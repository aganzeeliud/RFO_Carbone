The OEI Framework

Detailed technical specifications of our multi-sensor fusion approach to carbon monitoring and avoided emissions modeling.

Phase 1: Observation

Multi-Sensor Data Fusion

NASA GEDI Lidar

The Global Ecosystem Dynamics Investigation (GEDI) provides high-resolution laser ranging of Earth’s forests. We utilize L2A and L4A products to derive vertical canopy structure and aboveground biomass density (AGBD) at 25m resolution.

ESA Biomass CCI

The European Space Agency’s Climate Change Initiative (CCI) provides globally consistent biomass maps. We use these for cross-validation and to fill GEDI gaps, ensuring a wall-to-wall spatial coverage of the Okapi Reserve.

GFW Forest Change

Hansen et al. (Global Forest Watch) data provides annual tree cover loss alerts. This dataset is critical for identifying deforestation hotspots and calculating the 'Area Lost' variable in our emission models.

Carbon Sequestration Modeling

Our model follows the **IPCC 2006 Guidelines** for National Greenhouse Gas Inventories. We apply a carbon fraction (CF) of 0.47 to convert Aboveground Biomass (AGB) into Carbon (C).

The OEI Core Equation
C_total = Σ (Area_i × AGBD_i × CF)

Where Area_i is the pixel area, AGBD_i is biomass density from GEDI, and CF is 0.47.

To calculate Net Annual Sequestration, we account for the increment in forest growth minus the emissions from deforestation and degradation events detected by satellite alerts.

Validation Standards
  • IPCC Tier 3: High-resolution spatial modeling.
  • REDD+ VM0015: Methodology for avoided deforestation.
  • Verra Alignment: Ensuring data integrity for carbon credits.
Phase 2: Climate Impact

Avoided GHG Emissions (REDD+)

Baseline Scenario (BAU)

The "Business-As-Usual" (BAU) baseline is established by analyzing deforestation rates in a "Reference Region" outside the reserve with similar socio-economic drivers and forest types. This follows the Verra VM0015 methodology.

Project Scenario

The project scenario monitors the actual forest loss within the Okapi Wildlife Reserve. The difference between the BAU baseline and the Project scenario constitutes the Avoided Emissions.

Emission Factor Calculation

To convert carbon loss into CO2 equivalent (tCO2e), we use the molecular weight ratio of CO2 to C (44/12 ≈ 3.67).

Avoided tCO2e = (Loss_BAU - Loss_Project) × C_Density × 3.67

This calculation allows for the direct translation of forest conservation into global climate metrics compatible with the Paris Agreement.

Technical Deep Dive

The OEI Data Pipeline

1. Automated Ingestion

The pipeline begins with the automated retrieval of multi-temporal satellite data using the Google Earth Engine (GEE) Python API. We ingest:

  • GEDI L4A: Aboveground Biomass Density (AGBD) footprints.
  • Sentinel-2: MSI Level-2A surface reflectance for land cover classification.
  • Hansen GFC: Global Forest Change annual loss layers.

2. Pre-processing & Normalization

Raw data undergoes rigorous cleaning:

  • Cloud Masking: Utilizing the QA60 band to remove atmospheric interference.
  • Spatial Harmonization: Resampling all raster products to a consistent 30m UTM projection.
  • Filtering: Removing GEDI footprints with sensitivity scores below 0.9 to ensure biomass accuracy.

3. Spatial Analytics (Python Stack)

The heavy lifting is performed using a specialized Python environment:

  • GeoPandas: For vector-based boundary analysis of the Okapi Wildlife Reserve.
  • Rasterio/Xarray: For high-performance multidimensional array processing of biomass stacks.
  • Zonal Statistics: Aggregating biomass density across protected vs. non-protected strata.

4. Carbon Intelligence Modeling

Final metrics are generated through our proprietary OEI algorithms:

  • Sequestration: Calculating the delta in AGB over time based on Net Primary Productivity (NPP).
  • Emission Factors: Applying IPCC-standard conversion coefficients (3.67 for tCO2e).
  • REDD+ Baseline: Dynamic benchmarking against regional deforestation trends.
