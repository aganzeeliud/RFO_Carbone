Scientific Executive Summary

The OEI Protocol

A high-fidelity environmental monitoring framework designed to secure the climate integrity of the Congo Basin's most critical carbon sinks.

Phase 1: Precision Observation

How we "see" the forest from space using advanced laser and satellite technology.

Lidar Analysis

We use NASA GEDI (a powerful laser on the Space Station) to "scan" the forest. It measures the height and density of every tree with incredible detail, much like a 3D X-ray of the jungle.

NASA Space Assets
Multi-Sensor Fusion

One satellite isn't enough. We combine GEDI's lasers with Sentinel-2 (optical cameras) and ESA Biomass (radar) to create a complete, gap-free map of the entire 1.37 million hectare reserve.

Why this matters: By using three different types of "eyes" (Laser, Camera, and Radar), we can see through clouds and map areas that traditional systems miss.
02

Phase 2: Carbon Quantification

Turning forest maps into scientific carbon measurements.

Stock Integrity Algorithm

C_stock = Σ (A_i × AGBD_i × 0.47)

We calculate the "Carbon Stock" by taking the forest area (A) and its biomass density (AGBD), then applying the IPCC global standard (0.47) to find the actual carbon weight.

Avoided_GHG = ΔLoss × Density × 3.67

We calculate "Avoided Emissions" by measuring how much forest *wasn't* lost compared to unprotected areas, converting that saved forest into prevented CO2 emissions.

Global Standards
  • IPCC 2006: The gold standard for greenhouse gas reporting.
  • REDD+ VM0015: Official rules for calculating "saved" forest emissions.
  • Verra Standard: Ensuring our data is accurate enough for carbon credits.

Stage 1: Multi-Sensor Data Collection

**What happens:** Our system automatically connects to Google Earth Engine to pull down the latest satellite data.

**Why it matters:** We need fresh data every year to see changes in the forest canopy as they happen.

Stage 2: Pre-processing & Cloud Masking

**What happens:** We use an algorithm called S2Cloudless to "wipe away" clouds from the pictures, leaving only clear forest images.

**Why it matters:** The Congo Basin is very cloudy; without this, our data would be blocked by weather.

Stage 3: Spatial Fusion & Modeling

**What happens:** We use Machine Learning (Random Forest) to teach the computer how tree height (from lasers) relates to tree color (from cameras).

**Why it matters:** This allows us to map the biomass density of every single 10-meter square across the entire reserve.

Stage 4: Carbon Flux Calculation

**What happens:** We overlay our biomass map with Global Forest Watch data to see where trees were lost and calculate exactly how much carbon was released.

**Why it matters:** This identifies "hotspots" of forest loss so conservation teams can act quickly.

Stage 5: REDD+ Baseline Benchmarking

**What happens:** We compare the reserve's performance against the REDD+ "Business-As-Usual" baseline (what would have happened without protection).

**Why it matters:** It proves that the reserve is actually making a difference compared to surrounding areas.

Stage 6: Final Result Documentation

**What happens:** All findings are published as Open-Access Data and clear summaries for the public and policymakers.

**Why it matters:** Transparency ensures that our climate impact is verifiable and trusted by the global community.

Scientific Accountability

Our methodology accounts for both **deforestation** (complete conversion) and **degradation** (partial loss of canopy cover). This is particularly critical in the Congo Basin, where artisanal mining and selective logging often create significant carbon leakage that traditional monitoring systems overlook.
