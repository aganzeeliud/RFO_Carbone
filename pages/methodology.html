Stage 1: Multi-Sensor Data Collection

The pipeline triggers an automated pull from **Google Earth Engine (GEE)**, aggregating NASA GEDI L4A Lidar waveforms, ESA Sentinel-2 MSI optical bands, and ALOS-2 PALSAR-2 radar. This raw ingestion accounts for diurnal and seasonal variations across the Congo Basin.

Stage 2: Pre-processing & Cloud Masking

Raw imagery undergoes **QA/QC filtering**. Sentinel-2 data is processed using the *S2Cloudless* algorithm to remove atmospheric interference. GEDI footprints are filtered for sensitivity and beam strength, ensuring only high-confidence biomass measurements enter the synthetic model.

Stage 3: Spatial Fusion & Modeling

Using a **Random Forest Regression** model, we project GEDI's 25m LiDAR samples onto a continuous 10m grid provided by Sentinel-2. This creates a wall-to-wall Aboveground Biomass Density (AGBD) map of the reserve, filling the gaps between LiDAR tracks with high-fidelity spectral proxies.

Stage 4: Carbon Flux Calculation

The OEI Stock Algorithm calculates total carbon mass by multiplying pixel area by AGBD and applying the **IPCC carbon fraction (0.47)**. Annual forest loss (GFW) is then intersected with this biomass map to determine exact carbon emissions caused by deforestation.

Stage 5: REDD+ Baseline Benchmarking

Actual emissions are benchmarked against a **Verified "Business-As-Usual" (BAU)** baseline. This regional baseline is derived from historical deforestation rates (2000-2020) in non-protected areas of the Ituri landscape, establishing the "additionality" of the reserve's protection.

Stage 6: Final Result Documentation

Final analytics are exported into **Open-Access CSVs** and **Scientific Executive Summaries**. These documents provide a transparent, pixel-to-report audit trail, allowing external auditors to verify every metric shown on the OEI Intelligence Dashboard.

Scientific Accountability

Our methodology accounts for both **deforestation** (complete conversion) and **degradation** (partial loss of canopy cover). This is particularly critical in the Congo Basin, where artisanal mining and selective logging often create significant carbon leakage that traditional monitoring systems overlook.
